# Generic composition primitives

Use these as taste and context, not a locked recipe.

## Hierarchy

- Let the headline dominate without making supporting copy feel miniature.
- Limit line length so claims can be understood at a glance.
- Make the primary button wider, clearer, and higher contrast than secondary actions.
- Keep price and action visually connected.

## Rhythm

- Alternate dense explanation with simple visual proof or breathing room.
- Change surfaces to signal a new argument, not merely for decoration.
- Repeat the CTA after meaningful increases in conviction.
- Keep a deliberate path from promise to mechanism to evidence to offer to close.

## Tangibility

- Frame the actual interface, document, template, workflow, result, or conversation.
- Use labels and annotations to explain what a buyer is seeing.
- Prefer one legible demonstration over a wall of tiny screenshots.

## Surfaces

- Use semantic colors, disciplined borders, controlled radii, and restrained shadows.
- Reserve the strongest accent for action and the secondary accent for emphasis.
- Avoid decorative gradients, glass cards, random pills, and repeated card grids unless they serve the offer.

## Responsive transformation

- Preserve the promise and primary CTA in the first mobile viewport when practical.
- Collapse supporting detail before shrinking critical text below comfortable reading size.
- Stack comparisons in an order that preserves meaning.
- Keep payment controls away from container edges and adjacent wallet controls.
